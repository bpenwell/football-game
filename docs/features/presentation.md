# Presentation (graphics, camera, HUD, audio)

Feature-level doc: how a match looks and sounds for players **today** on `main`. Performance rules and budgets: `../optimization/README.md`. Layering (presentation reads sim state and owns no rules): `../design/architecture.md`. Open work: GitHub issues labelled `area:presentation` and `area:audio` (listed below).

## Summary
Matches are played in **Crater Bowl**, an original lunar stadium built inside a crater. Rigged CC0 athletes, a broadcast-style overlay and event effects (camera shake, tackle dust, touchdown fireworks) sit on top of a sim that knows nothing about them. The menus have a licensed music soundtrack. Matches have no sound effects yet.

The stadium screens show “PRIVATE MATCH LOBBY” while players are in the private lobby. When the
match starts, the screens show the score and the configured game clock.

**Scorebug.** The ball spot names the team whose territory contains the ball. The mini field reads
from the offense’s end toward the goal it attacks. Its cyan marker is the line of scrimmage and
its gold marker is the line to gain. The mini field appears during Classic scrimmage. Conversion
attempts read `TRY FOR N`. Kickoffs and the coin toss leave the down cell empty: they have no down
(#131). The period label follows the match rules, which use halves today. The play clock turns
orange-red at 5 s only when your team has the ball (#168). Training and practice show their drill
title under the bug, and the queue search shows there while you warm up.

**HUD layout (#116).** The scorebug sits on the bottom edge at one fixed width: down & distance
takes the slack, so the bug doesn't jump when the play-clock cell hides at the snap. The movement
meters sit to its left. The status line is a small backed pill on the scorebug's top-left edge, so
nothing floats over the controlled player. After the whistle it summarizes the play in player words
(`TACKLED · +3`, `SACK · -5`, `OUT OF BOUNDS · +7`, `INCOMPLETE`, `INTERCEPTION`), not the raw whistle
code. The play-call picker uses the menu theme and is pinned top-right, over the sky. Replay key
hints are a backed bar of key + action pairs just above the scorebug. Post-game, only the featured
player stands on stage while their stat card is up, and the hint is a backed pill in the corner.

**Tackle fall and get-up (#362).** View-only: the sim still spots the ball and frees the
athlete on its own clock; every client runs its own fall, and a replay's fall may differ slightly.
- **Hit:** on contact the carrier freezes for 0.07 s (hit-stop). Then his legs, spine and head
  become physics bones (`PhysicalBoneSimulator3D`, 6DOF joints with anatomical limits, so no joint
  bends backwards) and keep his run. One impulse (the tackler's closing velocity x 18) lands at the
  contact point, 0.55 yd up on the tackler's side, shared by the nearest bones. A low hit from the
  front trips him forward over it, a high one knocks the chest back, a hip hit spins him. Speed caps
  stop a bad solver contact from launching him. His arms stay out of the physics and clamp the ball
  to his chest.
- **Dead ball (tackle, sack):** he lies about 1 s, then gets up where he fell. The settled pose
  blends into the `LayToIdle` clip, so he rises without sliding. As he stands, the hug eases into the
  normal carry. Then he jogs back to the spot the sim has him at (usually 1–4 yd back, where forward
  progress stopped him), before the 2 s auto next play.
- **Fumble:** the stripped carrier ragdolls too and scrambles up at 0.45 s. The sim frees him after
  0.8 s, so the view runs about 0.3 s late and then catches up, a bit faster than the sim moves.
- **While he's down:** your ground ring and his blob shadow sit under the body, not the tackle spot.
  A teleport (the next snap, a replay seek) stands him up on the new spot at once.

**Teams (#233).** The league has eight original clubs, listed in `src/sim/teams.gd`: HELIOS (HEL),
NEBULA (NEB), ECLIPSE (ECL), REGOLITH (REG), SELENE (SEL), APOGEE (APG), QUASAR (QSR) and TYCHO
(TYC). Each has a name, a 3-letter code, primary and secondary colors and a logo
(`assets/teams/<id>.png`). Every match is still HELIOS (team 0) vs NEBULA (team 1): the host's
team picker is #234. The scorebug shows each team's logo between its color tab and its code, and
the hold-Tab scoreboard puts HELIOS's logo on the left and NEBULA's on the right. Logos on the
lobby, match intro and game overview are #235, and on the jumbotron #135.

**Coin toss cutscene (#129).** Every match with an opening kickoff starts with it (overtime reuses it once #16 lands); drills, Chaosball and `--no-kickoff` never show it. The match's athletes, ball, aim art and HUD hide behind it, and a letterboxed broadcast sequence plays on the real stadium:
- **0–1.85 s:** the two captains (each team's QB look) jog to midfield, where the referee waits. A lower third reads `COIN TOSS · CAPTAINS TO MIDFIELD` and `HELIOS VS NEBULA`.
- **1.85–3.0 s:** the ref flips a big two-faced coin (HELIOS gold `H`, NEBULA cyan `N`). It spins up against the dome and lands with the winner's face up.
- **3.0 s (the sim's `TOSS_REVEAL`):** the camera cuts to the coin and the crowd roars: excitement spike, fireworks and a short camera shake. The title reads `YOU WON THE TOSS` or `<TEAM> WINS THE TOSS`, while the winning captain jumps and the loser shakes his head.
- **Then:** the camera cuts to the captains. If you won, you get two cards, **RECEIVE** (*Your offense gets the ball first.*) and **KICK** (*Defense first. Ball after halftime.*), which you click (1 / 2 also work), with a bar draining over the 5 s window. Otherwise you see `<TEAM> IS CHOOSING…`.
- **The pick:** the card stays lit with `<TEAM> WILL RECEIVE` / `WILL KICK` under the title. It dips to black and fades back into the live kickoff, inside the sim's 0.9 s `TOSS_OUTRO`, so the kickoff starts with its full play clock.
- **Skip:** Space / Enter work once the result shows. If you won, skipping takes the default (receive). Otherwise it closes the cutscene and the HUD pill (`<TEAM> IS CHOOSING`) carries on. Esc stays the pause menu. Before the reveal, Space / J / the other keys do nothing, so nothing fires behind the scene.

**Audio today:** only the menu soundtrack. It plays `assets/music/tracks.json` in order, loops the playlist, and has a "now playing" widget (prev / pause / next). Credits are in `assets/music/CREDITS.md`. Volume follows the music and master settings. A match plays no SFX, crowd or whistle (#22).

## Design decisions

- Both skinned uniforms use a small culling margin around the imported bounds. The margin keeps the body visible when a close camera follows the knockback pose (#173).
- **Original look only.** It's a sci-fi lunar dome, never Gridiron's art or UI. Assets must be CC0 or compatible-license, logged in `ASSETS.md`.
- **Team logos are generated art (#233).** The owner asked for codex-made art, so Codex CLI image generation drew all eight from one shared style prompt (`tools/brand/team_logos_prompt.md`): circular flat badge, thick dark outline, no text, no real-team likeness. `tools/brand/team_logos.gd` keys out the white background and crops them to 512 px. SELENE was regenerated because a crescent with a star reads as a flag or religious symbol. A hand-drawn SVG set was the fallback and was not needed. Provenance is in `ASSETS.md`. The names avoid real pro and college nicknames: `tests/test_teams.gd` checks them against a deny list of space-themed ones (ROCKETS, ASTROS, COMETS, GALAXY…). Teams are pure data in `src/sim/teams.gd` (logo *paths* only), and `src/game/art/team_logos.gd` loads each texture once and caches it for every view. The PNGs are 512 px and imported with mipmaps. Their `.import` files are committed through a `.gitignore` exception (#260); a fresh checkout would otherwise re-import them without mipmaps. Without mipmaps the 34 px scorebug copy shimmers, and a 128 px copy would just be a second file to keep in sync. The scorebug's fixed width (#116) grows from 660 to 744 px for the two logos and their gaps. The movement meters still fit to its left at 1280×720. The overlay's `TEAM_NAMES`/`TEAM_ABBR` now come from the roster, and `set_teams(ids)` swaps them for #234. The end-zone text, POTG and game overview still hard-code HELIOS/NEBULA until #234 routes them through `Teams`; `test_teams.gd` checks they agree with the roster defaults.
- **The tackle fall is a ragdoll, not clips (#362).** The owner asked for these:
  - the hit has impact;
  - the carrier goes down protecting the ball;
  - no bones bend the wrong way;
  - the fall reacts to where he was hit.

  Canned clips can't react to the hit point. The arms stay out of the physics (athlete_pose
  `secure`), so the ball stays tucked whatever the body does. The fall comes only from where and how
  hard he was hit, never a random push. Joint limits are built in the rest pose (PhysicalBone3D
  resets to rest on entering the tree), so they are measured from standing straight, not from the
  hit frame.

  **Get-up:**
  - Easing the view back to the sim spot *while* he rises made him skate 1–4 yd (the owner flagged
    it), so he rises in place and jogs back after.
  - The rig's `root` bone must follow the clip. Holding it in world space while the view root moves
    slid the body during the blend.
  - The hug used to switch off at once as he stood, popping the ball from the chest to the hand
    (0.48 yd in a frame). It is now a weight that eases into the carry.
  - LayToIdle starts on his back. A prone body rolls over during the 0.3 s blend, which reads as
    pushing up.
- **Bodies turn, they don't snap; a standing body holds still (#423, #422).** The sim turns an athlete at once. Each tick `place()` moves the drawn facing `1 - exp(-TURN_K * DT)` (`TURN_K` 16) of the way to the sim's, so a 180° cut turns over about 0.2 s (an all-AI match had about 450 single-frame turns over 34°, 150 over 90°). A teleport (new play, replay seek) still snaps. Standing, `sync()` keeps aiming at the last wanted facing (`_want_yaw`): it used to read back the drawn rotation, which `interpolate()` had itself blended from the two targets, so a stopped player flipped between two facings every frame for as long as he stood (0.64 rad per frame in `facing_test`). `_process`'s own velocity turn now runs only for replays (no recorded facing, #209) and the jog back after a fall, where `interpolate()` doesn't set the facing. Tests: `tests/test_render_perf.gd` (a stop mid-turn settles and finishes the turn; a reversal turns at most 45° per tick and faces the new way within 20 ticks; a teleport snaps), `tests/visual/facing_test.gd` (captures `facing-mid-turn`, `facing-standing`; red on the old code with 180° in one frame and 0.64 rad per frame standing).
- **Presentation never owns rules.** Views mirror sim state and are interpolated per render frame, so the sim stays deterministic and testable headless.
- **Rigged athletes are the most expensive thing on screen** (about 8 ms each on WSL's GL path). Measure before adding bones or characters. Details: `../optimization/README.md`.
- **The helmet is fitted at runtime, in Head-bone space (#158).** `InnersceneHelmet.glb` as built by `tools/characters/build_uniforms.py` perched on the crown: its shell rode about 0.2 high and was 1.8x the skull's width. `football_uniforms.gd` scales it by 0.75 and drops it 0.106 so the shell's inside clears the crown by about 7 mm, covers the back of the head to the skull base and keeps every cranium vertex inside (median clearance about 1.4 cm). The helmet hangs directly on the `Head` `BoneAttachment3D`, not on the yard-sized gear holder: the holder's 1/MODEL_SCALE made the same fit 7% too small and pushed the scalp through the crown. Fixing it at runtime kept the GLB and Blender recipe untouched. Shrinking or hiding head vertices was not needed, because no cranium vertex reaches the shell in any sampled clip. The opponent uniform's helmet is skinned into its own mesh and was not changed.
- **No floating marker over your player (#141).** A spinning cyan chevron used to hover above the controlled athlete. The owner removed it: you can never switch athletes (your seat is fixed for the match, position changes are rejected and Tab only shows the scoreboard), so there is no switch to re-find yourself after. The team-colored ground ring stays as the "you" marker. #12 plans rings for every athlete, with yours kept distinct.
- **The camera frames you above the HUD; the HUD doesn't move (#128).** The follow camera looks at the aim point (your player + aim range downfield), so a long aim range, a dead-ball whistle or backpedalling toward the lagging camera used to drop your ring to y≈630–760 of 720, behind the scorebug (top ≈623). `_framed_look` now pitches the look down just enough that the ring's near edge sits no lower than `FRAME_FEET_MAX` (0.80 of the screen height, ~576 px at 720p, clear of the status pill). It's exact geometry from the camera's real position, so it holds for any `FOLLOW_CAM_OFFSET` (a #140 zoom only has to scale it), FOV and follow lag, and it's a no-op when the look already frames you. Rejected: moving or shrinking the scorebug (the #116 layout is the fix for the HUD side; #176/#216 edit it), and raising the follow camera (changes the stands-safety distance #140 depends on, and a far aim range still pushes you down).
- **Wheel zoom dollies the follow camera; it doesn't change the FOV (#140).** The zoom scales `FOLLOW_CAM_OFFSET` (up and back together), so the camera slides along the same 34° sight line and `_framed_look` keeps the ring above the scorebug at every level. Changing FOV instead would have fought aim-zoom (#109, which owns the FOV) and flattened depth at the far end. Limits: **0.6x** (3.6 m up, 5.4 m back: well clear of a 2 m athlete; closer and your own player hides the line in front of him) to **1.6x** (9.6 m up, 14.4 m back: further and your player gets too small to read). The stands can't swallow the camera at any level: the boom rises 0.67 m per m, faster than any stand (upper deck 0.65), and is already ~5 m up at the end-zone wall even from 2 yd out of bounds. 0.1x per notch (trackpads send fractions), snapped to 0.01, eased with `1 - exp(-10·dt)`. Math in `src/game/camera_zoom.gd`, input in `main.gd` `zoom_camera`.
  - **The wheel is ignored in the passer's POV aim**, rather than given its own clamp: the POV pitch is the throw's launch angle and its FOV is aim-zoom, so any wheel effect there would either move a throw or fight #109. Also ignored with the taunt wheel open (C), in menus, the lobby, replays and the coin-toss cutscene.
  - **Reset is a middle click** (press + release with under 6 px of drag), since a middle drag already orbits before the snap. Rejected: a Settings slider (a trip out of the match to undo a scroll; can be added if players ask).
  - **Saved in `user://settings.cfg`** (`[camera] zoom`) on every change; headless runs only write an explicit `--settings-file`, like Launch loads.
  - **Replays and cutscenes use the default framing.** The replay "Player" cam runs the follow camera at 1.0x; the other replay cams, POTG (`potg_stage.gd`) and the coin toss own their cameras.
- **POV sits back 5.4 yd, 2.8 high (#128).** At 4.2 back / 2.6 high the near back (#13, 4 yd across) was cut at the bottom-right edge. Wideouts 18 yd out are outside the POV frame on purpose; you find them by turning the aim. The passer's own ring stays below the HUD in POV: getting it up there means pitching the view down, and the POV pitch is the throw's launch angle, so the follow-camera rule doesn't apply (#228).
- **The coin toss is a cutscene, not a HUD prompt (#129).** The owner disliked `YOU WON THE TOSS · 1 RECEIVE · 2 KICK` and asked for a coin-toss scene with captains, a flip and a crowd shout. The cutscene is presentation only (`src/game/coin_toss.gd`). It follows the sim's `TOSS` state and timers (`kicking.md`), pins its timeline to `Match.toss_elapsed()` on the host and offline (within 0.15 s), and runs from the first `TOSS` snapshot on clients.
  - **Staging:** it reuses the potg stage's idea of pinned poses and a scripted camera per beat, but it plays in the match's own stadium: the crowd roar has to be the real crowd. The cutscene hides the 14 match athletes and adds 3 puppets (2 captains + ref), so it is cheaper than play: 78k tris, 3 skinned, 158 instances vs 204k / 14 / 226 at the opening kickoff (`tests/render_budget.gd`, 2026-11-26, WSL headless).
  - **Copy:** the choice copy says what each option means, never which key to press. The comment on #129 asked that the kick option be obvious without hints, so both options are cards and the window shows as a draining bar.
  - **No crowd sound:** matches have no audio path yet (#22), so the roar is visual only. The stinger belongs to #38.
  - **The HUD toss pill stays** (`YOU WON THE TOSS` / `<TEAM> IS CHOOSING`) for anyone who skips the cutscene.
- **HUD text refreshes at about 10 Hz.** Rebuilding scorebug strings every frame cost ~1 ms/frame (`../optimization/LOG.md`). Animated parts (throw bar, banners) still run per frame.
- **The live HUD carries only what you play by (#131).** The owner asked to drop everything else from the in-match HUD:
  - **No mode line** (`5V5 CLASSIC  ·  RANKED`) under the scorebug. You picked the mode before the match, so it moved to the top line of the hold-Tab scoreboard. Drill titles and the queue search still show there because they change what you're doing.
  - **No phase words in the down cell.** `KICKOFF` and `COIN TOSS` used to fill it. The cell is down & distance, and a kickoff has none, so it stays empty and the bug keeps its width. The Tab board still names the phase.
  - **The toss pill has no key hints.** It reads `YOU WON THE TOSS` or `<TEAM> IS CHOOSING`. The owner disliked `PRESS 1 TO RECEIVE OR 2 TO KICK` on the HUD. Removing it can't strand anyone: no answer in `TOSS_WINDOW` (5 s) means receive, the usual pick. The choice stays discoverable in the controls list (`1` "Extra point / toss: receive", `2` "Two-point try / toss: kick"). The coin-toss cutscene (#129) now owns the toss moment and its choice (above). We rejected keeping the hints until #129 lands: the owner asked for them gone now.
  - **The kicker keeps `PRESS J OR CLICK TO KICK`, and an offense with a kick called keeps `PUNT · PRESS K TO CHANGE`.** These pills are the only place a new player learns to kick, and they show only for the player who has to act. The kick meter (#68, `kicking.md`) shows power, not the key, so the pills stay. The return team's `GET READY TO RETURN` was narration with nothing to do, so it's gone.
  - **No `LOFT N°` readout** beside the QB crosshair. The view pitch is the loft, and the arc dots and landing reticle already show where the throw goes.
  - **The play clock only alarms on your own snap (#168).** Against bots it counts their 5 s huddle (#136), so the old "red at 5 s" rule painted it red for the whole presnap on defense. Red now means "your clock is running out".
- **The carry is a procedural arm pose, not a clip (#244).** No Quaternius clip has a football carry, and the run clips swing the hand at the hip, so the old pin (`hand_position() + basis.z * 0.08`, and `+z` is backward in Godot) left the ball inside the torso. `art/athlete_pose.gd` is a `SkeletonModifier3D`: after the clip poses the skeleton it runs two-bone IK on the right arm to a hand target on the chest (`TUCK_HAND`, elbow toward `TUCK_POLE`, both in `spine_03` space), blended by a weight `player.gd` smooths over ~0.2 s. A QB for whom `world.can_throw()` holds instead holds the ball at `HOLD_BALL` in front of the chest, each wrist beside it with palms turned in (`HOLD_HAND_BASIS`), and the ball sits there rather than in the right palm. A first version sent both hands to the chest with the ball still in the right palm, so the hands stacked on one side of it. Every clip clenches a fist, so a hand on the ball blends its finger joints 70% back toward the rig's open rest pose (`HAND_OPEN`), leaving a slight curl around the ball; a free hand keeps the clip's fist. `throw_release()` zeroes the weight so the throw clip is untouched. The ball sits in a palm socket (`BALL_SOCKET`, `hand_r` space). Two Godot 4.3 facts shaped the code: modifier results exist only during the skeleton update (read the bones at any other time and you get the plain clip pose), so the view pins the ball from `skeleton_updated` and caches the socket transform for `ball_transform()`; and a skeleton outside the tree refreshes global poses lazily, so tests call `force_update_all_bone_transforms()`. Replays don't record the holder, so the pose is off in replays. Rejected: authoring a tuck clip (needs Blender work and an `AnimationTree` with per-bone filters, and doesn't help the later head-look and reach work, #245/#246).
- **The football is one code-built mesh and one shader (#159).** `art/football.gd` lathes a profile `r = RADIUS * (1 - (z/HALF_LENGTH)^2)^0.8` (0.5 would be an ellipsoid; 0.8 brings the ends to a point) into a 20x14 mesh, long axis local Z, laces on +Y. `football.gdshader` draws the four seams, the laces (a centre lace and eight cross stitches over the top seam) and the stripes from the object-space position, anti-aliased with `fwidth`, so there is no texture or UV layout. The old ball was a `SphereMesh` stretched 1.75x in the node's scale plus three child meshes; every pose that set the scale to `ONE` (held, loose) drew a 0.3 m sphere, and its two default-tessellation `TorusMesh` stripes were ~8k triangles. Building the mesh at size means nothing sets the ball's scale. The sim keeps a grounded ball's centre at height 0, so the view lifts a loose or dead ball by `RADIUS` and lays it on its side. Rejected: a CC0 model (laces as geometry, but a texture and import settings to keep, #260, for detail the game camera can't see) and modelling the laces as boxes (extra instances for the same read).
- **Heads track the ball within neck limits (#245).** The same `athlete_pose.gd` modifier turns `neck_01` (40%) and `Head` (60%) toward the ball view, about the chest's up and left axes, so it works on any clip and in replays (the ball view is replayed). Only athletes with a reason to watch the ball look (`player.gd` `watches_ball()`): the defense reads it (snap, carrier, pass) unless engaged in a block; offensive linemen only on a loose ball within 15 yd; other offensive players for the snap, then only a pass or loose ball within 15 yd (they're running routes or blocking while it's held); nobody engaged in a block, the carrier, or anyone down, diving or celebrating. Yaw is clamped to ±80°, and past that the look fades to neutral by 120°, so a ball behind you never wraps the neck; pitch is clamped to −30°/+40°. `player.gd` smooths the look point and weight (`LOOK_K`), so a fast ball is followed, not snapped to; a jump of more than 4 yd in one frame (new spot, replay seek) does snap, so heads don't sweep across the field. Heads only turn when the athlete is big enough on screen to see it (`head_visible()`: about 25 yd for the follow camera, further for zoomed replay cameras), and who looks is decided at 10 Hz rather than every frame; together that brings the cost to about +30 µs/frame (`../optimization/LOG.md` perf-05 to perf-08). A first pass clamped yaw at 70° and faded by 110°: a wide receiver with the ball 90° to his side before the snap barely turned (35°), so the limit went up to 80° (60° turn at 90°).
- **Receivers get ready and reach for a pass (#246).** While a pass (not the snap: `world.snap_in_flight()`) is in the air, an athlete who watches the ball under the #245 rules and is within 6 yd of where it comes down through chest height (`player.gd` `catch_point()`, the landing-marker math at 1.4 yd) raises both hands into a ready stance, palms forward and open, once the ball is under 0.45 s away (`CATCH_LEAD`: ball distance over ball speed; the weight blend is ~99% in by the catch). Until then everyone keeps the run clip's arm pump. As the ball closes from 4.5 to 2.0 yd from the chest, the hands blend to either side of the ball, palms toward it (`athlete_pose.gd` `_catch()`, two-bone IK, so a ball out of reach gets a full arm, never a stretched one). 2.0 yd is where the sim's 1.2 yd ground catch radius actually takes the ball, so the arms are fully out as it snaps in. The arms do one job, the stronger of catch and carry, so at the catch the reach hands over to the #244 tuck. **Joint limits:** the upper body turns toward the ball (up to 35°, about the vertical, split over `spine_02`/`spine_03`); the reach is clamped to 75° either side of the chest's heading and fades back to the stance by 120°, so a ball from behind gets a turn and a ready stance, never arms bent backward; a ball more than 20–35° to one side after the turn is caught with the near arm only, the far arm staying in the stance; a ball more than 50–62° overhead (running under it) gets the near arm straight up, palm forward and fingers along the arm, while the other arm keeps the clip's arm pump. Palms face the ball's sides, turned slightly toward its back, with the fingers along the reach, for one hand or two: a palm facing straight down the reach bends the wrist back 90°+, like a push. All catch math uses an upright frame at the chest (its heading, world up): a sprinting chest leans about 40° forward, which put the stance at the hips and read overhead balls as behind. Visual only: catch rules are unchanged (#247, #9). What didn't work at first: the first version had no joint limits, so a ball from behind bent both arms backward at the shoulder and twisted the wrists (owner review); the snap is a ball flight too, so the QB and nearby linemen briefly took a catch stance after every snap; and a 0.6 yd full-reach distance left the arms half out at the catch; and raising the stance for the whole flight made defenders and receivers near the landing spot sprint with both hands up by the face for a second or more on a deep pass (user report), hence `CATCH_LEAD`.
- **Replays animate from the recording, not the live sim; blocks at speed run (user report: AI running with hands out).** A replay moves each view along recorded positions, but `player.gd` used to pick its clip and arm poses from the live athlete, frozen at the whistle: anyone still engaged in a block then played `Push` (both hands out) for the whole replay while running his route, anyone tackled slid along on his back, and a scorer's celebration flips played over his replayed run. `main._render_replay` now hands each view its recorded flags (`replay_flags`: 1 diving, 2 grounded; `sync()` resets it to -1 on the next live tick), and a replayed view picks only from those flags and its own motion (`_choose_replay_anim`): no block, fair-catch, celebration, spin or catch pose from the live sim. The recording now carries the block push, catch reach and fair-catch signal too (flag bits 4, 8, 16: #374, #198, see `replays.md`). Live, an engaged pair moving together at sprint pace (kick returns, downfield run blocks near the carrier, up to ~9.5 yd/s) also played `Push`; it now pushes only below `PUSH_MAX_SPEED` (5 yd/s, the jog/sprint split, +0.5 hysteresis), so a line block that grinds still pushes and a pair running together pumps its arms.
- **A block pushes with the upper body over running legs.** `Push` (Quaternius UAL) is a standing two-hand shove: played as the whole clip on a moving pair, the legs stood still and the athletes slid. `athlete_pose.gd` `_push()` now samples the clip's spine and arm rotation tracks (`PUSH_BONES`, `Animation.rotation_track_interpolate` at `push_time`, `PUSH_RATE` 1.3) over whatever leg clip plays (Idle/Jog), blended by `push_weight` (smoothed like the carry). Godot 4.3's `AnimationPlayer` has no per-bone mask, and an `AnimationTree` with filtered blends would have been a rebuild of every view for one layer. `Push` leans in from the pelvis, which stays on the leg clip, so `spine_02` also takes the pelvis difference: without it the arms pointed straight up. While pushing, the athlete faces his opponent (not his velocity), and one driven backward plays the jog in reverse: a backpedal (the pack has no backward clip). Before the face-to-face fix in the sim, a blocker could still engage a defender from behind while both ran the same way. Found with a scratch all-AI match logging every non-carrier moving above 3 yd/s with an arms-out pose: over 63 plays with a replay after each, 181 replay `Push` episodes and 283 live ones before, 0 and 113 after (the rest are defensive linemen driven back below 5 yd/s).
